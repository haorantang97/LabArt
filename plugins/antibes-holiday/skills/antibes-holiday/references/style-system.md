# Relaxed Black-Pen Line System

This system describes how the pen moves. It is not a motif library.

## Mature Reference Profile

The high-weight mature work reads as quick black-pen notation on plain paper:

- long contours launch decisively but may drift, lurch, overshoot, or lose their
  destination before recovering;
- curves contain changes in speed, tension, and direction large enough to alter
  the inferred structure, not only decorate its edge;
- short recognition marks behave like visual shorthand;
- line weight is mostly restrained but darkens at a few stops, overlaps, or retraces;
- intended junctions may miss, cross too far, or resume slightly displaced;
- anatomy and object construction may be assembled from fragments that do not
  fully agree in perspective or proportion;
- contours often stop where the viewer can complete them;
- local clusters can be busy while the surrounding page remains quiet;
- proportions are freely distorted around the action or recognition knot;
- residual partial marks sometimes remain near the main figure;
- the drawing stops before anatomy, objects, or space are fully resolved.

The relaxed effect does not come from random wobble, beige paper, or low accuracy.
It comes from confident incompletion plus visible loss and recovery of control.

## Structural Instability

This line system must not merely simplify a well-drawn contour. Its construction
should remain slightly unstable at the scale of the form.

Use a small number of consequential irregularities:

- one long contour drifts far enough to change the silhouette before recovering;
- one intended meeting misses, overlaps, or restarts several pen-widths away;
- one feature is built from two fragments that disagree slightly;
- one exploratory line remains confidently wrong instead of being erased;
- one slow knot accumulates ink while nearby contours stay thin and under-described.

Do not distribute these effects evenly. One broad lurch is more useful than
twenty tiny wiggles. The hand may seem lightly tipsy, but the observation remains
alert. Preserve readability through posture, action, and shorthand rather than
through clean anatomy.

## Stroke Anatomy

### Long sweep

Purpose:

- establish mass, posture, direction, or spatial envelope;
- carry the eye through the page;
- sometimes perform two jobs at once.

Behavior:

- launched as a single pass but allowed to lose its path;
- one broad lateral drift, abrupt correction, or uneven change of curvature;
- occasional visible lurch rather than only a shallow cosmetic kink;
- beginning may be blunt, tentative, false, or clearly overshot;
- ending often stops abruptly without decorative taper.

Avoid:

- mathematically smooth curves;
- constant-width vector paths;
- repeated digital jitter;
- correcting a drifting contour back into anatomical elegance;
- polishing the curve until all tension disappears.

### Shorthand fragment

Purpose:

- reveal identity, action, material, or state in very few marks.

Behavior:

- angular nick, hook, wavering edge, partial loop, or compressed zigzag;
- often detached from the contour it describes;
- may be thicker than a long sweep because the pen slows down;
- remains specific without becoming realistic.

Do not equate shorthand with facial features. It can describe a shell edge, valve, hinge, leaf fold, roof break, cable contact, or change of state.

### Recognition knot

Purpose:

- hold the highest information density;
- slow the eye briefly;
- explain what the drawing is doing.

Behavior:

- several short marks touch or nearly touch;
- one overlap or retrace may become visibly darker;
- small closure errors are acceptable;
- detail falls away quickly outside the knot.
- repeated correction belongs here; the rest of the drawing should retain single,
  imperfect attempts rather than hairy duplicate contours.

Examples of knots include contact, grasp, opening, mechanism, impact, exchange, or transformation. They are not fixed objects.

### Residual search mark

One to four incomplete or corrective marks may remain in an expressive
illustration when they:

- preserve the feeling of active discovery;
- echo the direction of the main gesture;
- suggest a neighboring form or abandoned continuation.

Delete them when they look like decoration, motion icons, evenly distributed
roughness, or a generic sketch overlay. Logos and very small marks may need fewer.

## Tempo

A successful drawing contains at least three states:

1. `launch`: a fast structural sweep with low information density;
2. `loss`: a drift, lurch, missed meeting, displaced restart, or false path;
3. `recovery`: compact shorthand and retracing at the recognition knot.

The transition between these states creates relaxation. Uniformly careful drawing
feels stiff; uniformly loose drawing feels careless; clean fast lines plus neat
details feel like professional editorial sketching.

## Completion

Use this stopping order:

1. action or relationship is readable;
2. dominant mass is inferable;
3. recognition knot is specific;
4. stop.

Do not continue merely to:

- close silhouettes;
- make both sides symmetrical;
- correct every proportion;
- explain hidden anatomy;
- align fragments;
- distribute detail evenly;
- make the result look professionally finished.

## Contour Discipline

Omission must affect construction, not only decoration:

- avoid a single continuous contour that explains head, torso, limb, and clothing
  in one competent chain;
- let each mark carry a role: gesture, recognition, contact, correction, or release;
- spend detail on one recognition knot rather than distributing proof of drawing
  competence across the form;
- give supporting figures enough crooked specificity to read as observed people or
  things, not generic sticks or pictograms;
- vary omission by subject and action instead of enforcing a percentage or line count;
- when the image remains readable after removing a descriptive line, remove it.

Never compensate for excessive description by adding more wobble, and never
compensate for clean lines by deleting the subject until only symbols remain.

## Subject Transfer

### Living forms

Begin with center of gravity and movement. Preserve one distinctive anatomical
rhythm, but allow one structural mismatch where two fragments do not fully agree.
Omit anatomy that does not affect the action. A living form should not resolve
into a graceful fashion figure or academically competent gesture drawing. A face
usually needs only a profile break, hair mass, tilt, or one directional mark.

### Objects

Begin with how the object operates. Distort or omit exterior casing while preserving the opening, joint, pressure point, feed path, or contact surface.

### Places

Begin with the route through the place. Use boundary, threshold, and one scale cue. Avoid scenic filling.

### Abstract relationships

Choose a concrete physical proxy before drawing. A cord, ring, opening, load,
branch, break, fold, barrier, route, or transfer can make the relationship
observable without becoming a generic icon.

Then translate language into physical behavior:

- conflict -> incompatible line tempos or opposing tension;
- clarity -> a release into one calm movement;
- overload -> compressed marks with blocked escape;
- exchange -> a mark changes state while crossing a gap;
- growth -> repeated structure changes interval or scale;
- isolation -> one fragment loses relational contact.

The result should feel observed and drawn, not diagrammed. If viewers need the
prompt to understand what the free-floating lines mean, replace them with a more
legible physical proxy. Avoid arrows, boxes, labels, and familiar idea icons.

## Material

Target:

- black fine pen, gel-pen-like, or fineliner-like mark;
- plain warm-white or white uncoated paper;
- subtle paper tooth and mild uneven exposure visible at close range;
- minor ink pooling at selected stops;
- no pencil shading, brush calligraphy, marker fill, or digital outline effect.

For clean digital delivery, preserve the stroke edge, mild paper irregularity, and
pressure behavior while removing heavy camera cast, page borders, shadows, and
excessive grain. Do not polish the page until the line resembles archival editorial art.

## Composition

- Favor a vertical or lightly asymmetric field.
- Let the subject occupy only as much space as its gesture needs.
- Keep a large quiet release around the recognition knot.
- Allow one sweep or residual mark to approach an edge.
- Avoid geometric centering and perfectly balanced margins.
- Do not use a fixed negative-space percentage as a substitute for judgment.

## Story Scene Grammar

A complex scene is not a collection of fully drawn objects. It is a causal chain
distributed across the page.

### Narrative roles

- `Principal actor`: carries the largest change, desire, or reaction.
- `Counterforce`: resists, receives, interrupts, or redirects the action.
- `Narrative hinge`: the prop, contact, reveal, or impact where the event turns.
- `Causal path`: the long line, reach, gaze, baseline, spacing, or displaced object
  that lets the eye reconstruct the sequence.

The principal actor does not need to be the largest figure. The hinge may be
physically small but visually dense.

### Information hierarchy

- Use high detail only at the narrative hinge and one recognition point per actor.
- Let supporting figures collapse into crooked posture plus one distinctive
  fragment; do not turn them into clean pictograms.
- Keep environmental detail to the minimum needed for contact, scale, or direction.
- Separate actors with blank paper rather than background objects.
- Let one long contour belong simultaneously to an actor and the event path.

Complexity should come from relationships among sparse parts. Adding more complete
figures, furniture, architecture, texture, or perspective lines usually weakens
the scene.

### Non-equilibrium proportion

Distort scale according to narrative pressure:

- enlarge the feature that carries attitude, force, shelter, obstruction, or identity;
- compress a distant or secondary actor into a few marks even when literal perspective
  would make the scene less extreme;
- let one mass dominate the field while a much smaller counter-event activates the
  remaining space;
- crop or push a dominant contour toward an edge instead of recentering it;
- allow the densest detail to sit away from the geometric center.

Use one main proportional violation per image. If every component is exaggerated,
the hierarchy disappears.

### Empty space as time

Blank paper can encode:

- delay before contact;
- social or physical distance;
- anticipation around a suspended object;
- the travel time of a thrown, poured, falling, or transferred thing;
- release after a dense interaction.

Do not treat every gap as a compositional flaw. Fill a gap only if causality is
unreadable without another mark.

### Composition families

Choose by story logic, not by visual habit:

1. `Opposed exchange`: actors face inward from separate masses; a compact hinge
   sits between them.
2. `Scale-distance stage`: one large near actor anchors an edge while a tiny
   counteractor or event occupies a remote field; a sparse baseline or reach
   establishes their relationship.
3. `Dominant silhouette`: one enlarged feature or mass breaks expected proportion,
   crop, or balance; recognition detail stays compact beneath or beside it.
4. `Causal cascade`: one long sweep carries the eye from an initiating action to
   displaced props or a consequence in another region.

These are generative families, not templates. Change orientation, actor count,
edge, scale ratio, hinge position, and causal direction. Never copy a reference
layout.

### Scene failure modes

Reject a scene when:

- every actor has equal size, finish, and detail;
- a centered triangular arrangement makes the event feel staged;
- realistic perspective suppresses the important action;
- the setting is more described than the cause and consequence;
- motion lines or arrows substitute for an observable event path;
- empty space has been filled decoratively;
- the image reads as unrelated icons rather than one event;
- a dramatic accessory appears without being required by the new story.
- every body part and object edge joins correctly;
- the figures read as elegant trained sketches rather than unstable notation.

## Color

- Start with black or near-black ink.
- Add at most one flat spot color after the monochrome drawing works.
- Put color at the recognition knot or state change.
- Never use gradients or paper tint as the main style signal.

## Logo Translation

The illustration master and production mark solve different problems.

### Expressive master

- preserves pressure variation, small kinks, and open terminals;
- used at display size;
- may remain high-resolution raster or carefully authored vector.

### Production master

- preserves gesture and asymmetry;
- simplifies only unsafe gaps and microscopic texture;
- optically adjusts stroke width for target sizes;
- does not turn into a thick rounded app icon.

## Anti-Anchoring

Recurring subject matter is not style evidence. Before finalizing, check:

- Did an accessory appear only because it occurred in the references?
- Did the model add a face because it associates loose line art with characters?
- Did a familiar icon replace the intended relationship?
- Does the drawing still feel related when the subject changes?
- Are the lines alive without paper texture?

Delete imported motifs, not useful irregularity.

## Reference Safety

Never:

- trace or vectorize a source work;
- preserve the same subject, pose, prop arrangement, or contour path;
- reuse a recurring character, signature motif, handwriting, or logo;
- prompt with a source identity;
- package screenshots as skill assets;
- claim the transferable system is a third party's official style.

Use reference crops only during analysis and testing. Final outputs must use new subjects and compositions.
