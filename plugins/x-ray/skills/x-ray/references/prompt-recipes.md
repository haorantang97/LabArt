# Prompt Recipes

Keep one imaging block fixed while testing content changes. Start with a
projection and polarity appropriate to the actual brief rather than packing the
prompt with stylistic synonyms.

## Imaging Block

An integrated transmission radiograph of [SUBJECT], viewed in [PROJECTION].
Use [PALE FIELD / BLACK FIELD] with stronger attenuation [DARKER / BRIGHTER]
throughout. Thin [MATERIAL] is faint; thicker paths and overlapping [STRUCTURES]
accumulate attenuation. Dense [PARTS] can obscure detail. Show an intact silhouette
with plausible internal construction. Use restrained contrast and [CHOSEN
ACQUISITION TEXTURE]. Suppress visible-light reflections, glass refraction, lit
surface relief, cutaway edges, captions, and decorative effects.

## Textile

Specify which layers should overlap: interfacing, folded sleeves, cuffs, pocket,
front/back plackets, and seams. Ask for density changes at folds, not an ordinary
see-through garment under studio light.

## Mechanical Object Or Bag

Name only plausible internals and their connections. Preserve outer form,
fastenings, and contents in the same projection. State where dense material hides
detail. Do not request every mechanism to be perfectly legible through every layer.

## Living Action

Define one observable instant and its contacts. Then specify coherent joints and
near/far overlap. Add local motion only where warranted. Do not use blur to hide
structural uncertainty, nor demand anatomy accuracy without suitable evidence.

## Reference-Conditioned Change

Use [IMAGE] as the inspected appearance/geometry reference. Preserve projection,
polarity, contrast, texture, and unaffected structures. Change only [VARIABLE].
Describe the expected visible consequence: a contact change, extra attenuation,
overlap, or motion spread. Avoid a chain of consecutive edits for independent tests.

## Discriminating Stress Tests

- Dense material: place a ring across two contrasting regions, allowing its central
  hole and its blocked area to be assessed. A ring concentric with a reel can hide
  a failed edit.
- Mixed materials: overlap thin/folded textile and metal; judge textile appearance
  separately from dark metal silhouettes.
- Acquisition motion: spread all parts of a moving rigid object consistently;
  background remains stationary and there are no discrete full-object copies.
- Coupled action: trace hands, tools, limbs, and mechanisms before judging the
  overall dynamic composition.

Repeat relevant conditions when reproducibility is the goal. These scaffolds
do not specify a universal exposure, seed, model strength, or guaranteed outcome.
