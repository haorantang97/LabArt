DISTILLED STYLE KERNEL
Runtime contract: contains no source pixels, source glyphs, artist identity, copied coordinates, fixed composition statistics, font sample, or character-specific exception.

STRUCTURAL SKELETON
- Preserve exact character identity while redistributing assembly scale and leverage.
- Give different neighboring characters different structural roles: beam, tower, cavity, knot, or suspended remainder.
- A beam may extend far beyond ordinary balance; a vertical support may stop early or descend abruptly.
- Offset assemblies, crooked enclosures, compressed voids and sudden open cavities must remain causally constructed and legible.
- Awkwardness comes from deliberate structural disagreement, never from global rotation, wobble, erosion, or random damage.

ASSEMBLY AND RHYTHM
- Make adjacent width, height, center, density and internal void conspicuously unequal.
- Keep components within one character even when they crowd, drift or disagree in axis.
- Prefer blunt, angular and reluctantly resolved turns over polished curves and fluent linking.
- Do not let every character receive the same distortion recipe.

BRUSH CAUSALITY
- Use real soft-brush logic: loaded press, resistant drag, controlled lift, bone-thread travel, dry break and recovered contact.
- Keep saturated dark brown-black masses and sparse dry passages in the same work.
- Dryness follows travel direction and pressure loss; it is not a texture filter.
- Edges can fray locally, but the whole sheet must not become uniformly fuzzy or artificially aged.

RELATIONAL COMPOSITION
- Treat the layout as a field of pressure, not a grid. Vertical reading order survives while axes bend and starts, advances and endings disagree.
- Let some neighbors press or intrude and others retreat or hang. Preserve both local congestion and active paper.
- Do not regularize colored fields into private cells, straight gutters, repeated rows or poster alignment.

FAILURE SIGNATURES
- Ordinary fluent running-regular calligraphy with rough ink is a failure.
- Textbook clerical, seal, regular or cursive style used as one uniform template is a failure.
- Equal-sized centered characters, stable row bands, clean column gutters and decorative flying-white are failures.
- A printed font made rough, rotated, stretched or eroded is a failure.
