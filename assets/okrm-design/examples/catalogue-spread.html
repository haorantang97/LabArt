A storage jar with a short, upright neck and a body that swells to its widest point just below the shoulder. The slip was applied by dipping; the line where the maker's fingers held the foot is left uncovered, and the glaze pooled there in firing. Two kiln scars on the shoulder show that it was stacked.

The jar was sold with its lid missing and has never been given one. Its weight and the thickness of the wall suggest a workshop that made for the local market rather than for shipping. Of the sixty vessels in this record it is the only one whose place of making is known to within a few kilometres, because the clay was tested in 1988.
